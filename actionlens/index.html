ActionLens

Diagnosing Spatial-Temporal Binding Failures in Vision-Language Models

Gueter Josmy Faure1, Min-Hung Chen2, Hao Ping Wang1, Timothée Lardy1, Hung-Ting Su1, Winston H. Hsu1
1National Taiwan University, 2NVIDIA
arXiv Code Dataset

ActionLens evaluates whether vision-language models can bind actions to the right actor across spatial and temporal dimensions.

Abstract

Video question answering systems often select frames or score scene-level recognition independently, yet downstream answerers must bind the right action to the right person at the right moment. Current vision-language models (VLMs) achieve high scores on general video benchmarks, but systematically fail at spatial-temporal binding.

We introduce ActionLens, a diagnostic benchmark of 6,701 multiple-choice questions that decomposes spatial-temporal binding into five targeted capabilities: transition detection (D1), actor-specific identification (D2), concurrent action binding (D3), directed interaction reasoning (D4), and gaze detection (D5). Ground-truth answers are derived deterministically from 1.58 million per-second, per-person annotations without language model generation.

Evaluating 20 VLMs reveals a substantial human–model gap: humans achieve 91.0% pooled accuracy, whereas the leading open model reaches 65.6%. In multi-person scenes, up to 53% of model errors succumb to the binding trap—attributing the wrong actor's action. Furthermore, removing visual bounding box overlays in favor of text coordinates degrades accuracy by up to 19.2% on actor disambiguation.

ActionLens provides a reproducible diagnostic probe to evaluate and isolate fine-grained binding failures across current and future video architectures.

Key statistics

  • 6,701 multiple-choice questions with 100% deterministic ground truth derived from dense AVA annotations
  • 5 targeted diagnostics: Transition Sensitivity (194), Actor Disambiguation (2,000), Concurrent Action Binding (2,000), Directed Interaction Reasoning (1,611), and Gaze Detection (896)
  • 20 vision-language models evaluated from 4B to 38B parameters alongside proprietary models
  • 91.0% pooled human reference vs. 25.4% random guess baseline

How to run

ActionLens is integrated into lmms-eval. Run evaluation with:

git clone -b add-actionlens-dataset https://github.com/joslefaure/lmms-eval.git
cd lmms-eval
pip install -e .

accelerate launch --num_processes=1 -m lmms_eval   --model vllm   --model_args pretrained="Qwen/Qwen3.5-27B-Instruct",tensor_parallel_size=2   --tasks actionlens   --batch_size 1   --output_path ./results/actionlens_qwen35_27b
